# The Content Creator's Quickstart Guide to Cybersecurity and Internet Safety

In recent years, there's been an explosion of people wanting to become content creators: streamers, TikTokers, influencers, etc. But you may be surprised to learn that _even this seemingly-benign career choice can come with risks: stalkers, trolls, and haters abound._ If you plan to pursue a career in the spotlight, you'll want to take some extra precautions to protect yourself.

## Step 1: Picking an Identity

### Name

**I strongly discourage any aspiring public figure from using their real names.** This goes for any kind of public-facing persona. This is especially important if you have a very unique name or a name with a unique spelling.

Depending on the industry you wish to enter, the type of name you can pick varies wildly.

If you're planning to be a streamer or online figure, you can easily get away with using a handle or username as your identifier.

Other fields may require a more realistic sounding name. I personally used Behind the Name's [Random Name Generator](https://www.behindthename.com/random/) to invent and entirely fake name, but some people prefer going by a nickname (ex, "Nick" instead of "Nicholas"), a middle name, or any other such name. All options have their pros and cons. 

### Location

If you plan to need a public mailing address for this identity (such as to receive gifts from fans), I recommend renting a PO Box or private mailbox. Depending on local laws and company policy, you may need to register a legal entity or provide additional paperwork to receive mail in another name. Be sure to ask about this.

If you live in a small town and don't want your audience to know this, be sure to get a PO Box in a nearby town, ideally a major town with a large population that you're willing to make the trip to regularly.

You could also change or fuzz other details about yourself. For example, instead of saying you're from San Antonio, you can say you're from Central Texas, which could also include Austin and the surrounding smaller towns. (This is especially important if you're from a small town as mentioned before.)

You could take things a step further and lie entirely. For example, you could claim to be from New York. However, this requires extra effort.

1. You need to keep your story straight. This could include visual information (like a Yankees hat in the background), PO Box address, social media posts (not saying today was a beautiful day when it was a blizzard out) and more.
2. If you want to truly convince people, you'll need to be able to "pass" to other natives: you'll need to know the slang, the locations, the local events, etc.

### Miscellaneous

Try to avoid giving out additional details such as your exact age or celebrating your real birthday.

These may seem like small things, but a dedicated stalker could piece all of this information together to not only track you down, but confirm that they're on the right track.

When it comes to protecting yourself online, **less is more.**

Fewer accounts not only means less work to keep them updated, but also less risk of data breach or accidentally sharing something you didn't want to.

**Before you start building your online presence, ask yourself what platforms or support methods you _really need_ and stick to just those ones.**

### Getting Paid

While it's never a bad idea to diversify, don't stretch yourself too thin.

In addition to being more work to keep up with, **services like Patreon and Ko-Fi also present a privacy/cybersecurity risk**: they require your real name and information, and if they ever suffer a data breach it may be possible to correlate that data with your persona.

Also be mindful of how these platforms display your data. For example, until recently Venmo did not provide a way to hide your transactions or friends list at all. Even now, these are visible by default.

We'll talk about changing the settings of your accounts in [Step
3](#step-3-secure-your-accounts).

### Avoiding Impersonation

When you pick your platforms, provide a way to verify which ones are really you to fans. A common example is Linktree, or a website listing every platform you're using (Twitch, YouTube, Patreon, etc). This helps fans know if someone is impersonating you or is legitimiate.

(Personally I recommend a website - you have a lot more ownership of your data and flexibility. Regardless, make sure it's something your fans know they can trust.)

### Compartmentalization

**Do NOT reuse your existing personal accounts.** Sign up for all new ones using this new identity.

I have several suggestions to help keep your two identities separate in the event of a data breach while also maintaining convenience.

1. I encourage you to switch to an [encrypted email provider](/guides/moderately-important/email). I'll talk about why in [Step 3](#step-3-secure-your-accounts).
2. Use an [email aliasing service](/guides/moderately-important/email-aliasing). If you use the same email address everywhere (including in your personal life) your persona can easily by to your real identity. An email aliasing service will give you a near-infinite number of email addresses you can use to compartmentalize your various identities, but they'll all forward straight to your regular inbox for easy management.
3. Use a [Voice-over-IP](/guides/less-important/voip) phone number. You may already be familiar with these in the form of Google Voice. Google Voice is convenient because it simply can forward calls and messages to your existing number, but there are plenty of other options out there that offer better privacy and additional useful features.

Many websites like Discord will not accept a VoIP number for verification. There are ways around this such as getting an eSIM or prepaid SIM from a service like [Mint Mobile](https://www.mintmobile.com/) or [Visible Wireless](https://www.visible.com/). However this is also a good opportunity to ask if you really _need_ these services in your life.

In addition to being a dead-simple way to [track someone](https://www.vice.com/en/article/nepxbz/i-gave-a-bounty-hunter-300-dollars-located-phone-microbilt-zumigo-tmobile), your SIM phone number can also be taken over in a [SIM-swapping attack](https://www.trendmicro.com/en_us/what-is/cyber-attack/types-of-cyber-attacks/sim-swapping-scams.html), thereby allowing an attacker to get all your phone calls, text messages, etc, which often helps them access your online accounts.

### Passwords & 2FA

It would be awful to build up a loving, dedicated audience and then lose access to your accounts to someone who tries to take advantage of your fans - posting spam, scams, or simply just content you don't agree with. This could be devestating to your community and brand.

Start with [strong passwords](/guides/most-important/passwords). Use a password manager and be sure that you've got strong, unique passwords on every site.

Once you've done that, harden all your accounts and make them nearly unhackable by using [two-factor authentication](/guides/most-important/mfa) (2FA).

Doing these two things will make all your accounts nearly unhackable.

### Settings

You should also be sure to check the settings of your accounts:

* what profile information is shared publicly by default?
* What settings can you restrict or avoid filling out to further protect your privacy?

Remember not to stop at your public-facing accounts - like Discord or Twitch - but also secure your behind-the-scenes accounts like your email, any sponsorship sites, etc.

I strongly encourage you to practice this stuff in your personal life, too.

### Email

Use [encrypted email](/guides/moderately-important/email). Traditional email providers - like Gmail - can read your inbox. This means that if your email provider ever suffers a data breach - or gets compromised by a [rogue employee](https://www.lawinsider.com/dictionary/rogue-employee) - your email contents can be exposed. That includes bank statements, event reminders, contracts, or even personal correspondence. This is not just a possibility, it's actually [happened](https://www.theverge.com/2019/10/1/20893462/yahoo-engineer-hacking-accounts-stolen-nude-photos-videos-guilty-plea). Encrypted email providers store your emails in such a way that nobody - not even the employees - can access your inbox.

## Step 4: Online Data

### Family & Friends

When you start your new venture you may be tempted to share with friends and family. This may be a good idea, but know that they are also a weak spot. For example, your family may not remember to use your fake name when they talk about you, or they may share your content online with comments like "my son/daughter/brother/sister/etc did this!" However, even this can be a risk. A potential stalker who sees your parent's status and Googles them may find your information through their list of relatives.

The best defense against this is to stress how you want people to refer to you. Remind them that you're building a brand. It's not just about privacy, it's about making sure people know how to find you and follow your content. Using myself an example, I don't want people to follow Nate, I want them to follow The New Oil. That's where the real content is.

### Mod Tools

Another set of tools available to you here are moderation tools. Sites like Twitch and YouTube allow you to block certain words, for example. This is the perfect place to enter your real name and address, as well as any other information you wish to hide. Anyone attempting to [dox](https://www.avast.com/c-what-is-doxxing) you will either not be able to send the message, or the message will be invisible to others.

However, it's worth considering that this may also backfire by confirming to them that they have the real information.

As you grow, let your community moderators and other staff members know how to respond to such attempts. Maybe they should spam the chat with messages until your data is offscreen. Or perhaps they should lie and say that they know your real name and that isn't it. There are many options.

### Data Removal Services

Consider using a [data removal service](/guides/moderately-important/people-search/) to help reduce your odds of being doxed by removing your personal data from public searches. These tools are not bulletproof, and will require you to pay a subscription fee (or invest the considerable time to do it yourself), but if you have the resources to put into them, they will almost certainly reduce the odds of your data being found so easily.

## Have Fun

I hope that you found this page inspiring and empowering rather than scary and overwhelming.

There's a lot more you can do to protect yourself from all kinds of tracking, but this information should get you off to a solid start. Using these tools and techniques, you'll be able to defend against most basic attackers - the "[script kiddies](https://www.techtarget.com/searchsecurity/definition/script-kiddy-or-script-kiddie)" who are just bored or angry and looking to mess with someone, as well as the unsophisticated stalkers and doxxers who have very little in the way of time or resources to truly unmask your identity and harass you further.

With those threats off your plate, I hope that your mind is at ease and you're free to focus more time and attention on building your brand, your community, and enjoying your work. And by all means, if you have any questions or want to learn more, don't hesitate to [contact me](/links#contact).
